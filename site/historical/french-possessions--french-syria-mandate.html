Summary

The Mandate for Syria and the Lebanon was a League of Nations mandate established after World War I that included the territories of present-day Syria, Lebanon, and parts of Turkey. The mandate was intended as a temporary trusteeship until the inhabitants were ready for self-government, leading to the creation of several states. The French administered the region through various governments and territories until 1946, when the mandate ended, and Syria and Lebanon declared independence.
