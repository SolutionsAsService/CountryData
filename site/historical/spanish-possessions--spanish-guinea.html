History

18th-19th Centuries
Spain established colony in 1778 via Treaty of El Pardo. British base on Bioko (1827–1843). Spanish rule restored in 1844 as 'Territorios Españoles del Golfo de Guinea'.
20th Century
Treaty of Paris in 1900 defined territorial boundaries. Spanish Guinea became a union of colonies in 1926, a Spanish colony in 1956, and a province in 1958 until independence in 1968.
Agricultural Economy
Large cacao plantations developed on Fernando Po. Labor shortages led to the importation of workers from Liberia, Nigeria, and Cameroon.
Decolonisation
After WWII, Spanish Guinea moved towards decolonisation. Independence granted as Republic of Equatorial Guinea on 12 October 1968.

Modern Territory

Today part of Equatorial Guinea
