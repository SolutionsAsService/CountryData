Historical Context

Spanish West Africa Formation
Between 1946 and 1958
Ifni War And Aftermath
During the Ifni War, Ifni and the Sahara became separate provinces.
Decolonization Pressure
Spain relinquished its Saharan possession following Moroccan demands and international pressure.

Spanish Period

Initial Claim
At the Berlin Conference, Spain declared a protectorate from Cape Blanc to Cape Bojador.
Resistance
Spanish forces were challenged by stiff resistance from indigenous Sahrawi tribes.
Treaty Of Idjil
In 1886, Spain signed the Treaty of Idjil with the Emirate of Adrar.

Modern History

Moroccan Claims
After gaining independence in 1956, Morocco laid claim to Spanish Sahara.
Ifni War
The Moroccan Army of Liberation nearly occupied Ifni in 1957.
Polisario Front
In 1973, the Polisario Front was formed, challenging Spanish rule.

Present Status

UNStatus
Western Sahara is listed by the United Nations as a non-decolonized territory.
Population
Moroccan settlers currently make up more than two thirds of the inhabitants.
Legal Status
Under international law, Western Sahara is not a legal part of Morocco.
